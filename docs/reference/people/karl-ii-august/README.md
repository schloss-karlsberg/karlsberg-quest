# Karl II. August, Duke of Pfalz-Zweibrücken (1746–1795)

Reference for the speaker `herzog` (stylized 3D figure, codex). Full name Karl II. August Christian, Pfalzgraf von Birkenfeld-Zweibrücken. He ruled from 1775 to 1795. In the game period (1778–1793) he is **31 to 46 years old**.

Licence and credit for every image: [`sources.json`](sources.json). The images are not MIT-licensed. Read [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) first.

> **Do not use the brewery brand's label portrait.** The brewery brand puts a portrait of the duke on its bottles. Do not copy, trace or echo that label art, and do not name the brand anywhere in the game. Build the figure from the historical portraits below.

## Appearance by portrait

### 1. Brandt, c. 1775: young duke in state armour (age c. 29)
- **Face:** front view, turned slightly left. Round, smooth, still fairly slim. Light blue eyes, fine arched brows. Straight nose. Small, closed, slightly smiling mouth. Pale skin with pink cheeks. A first hint of the later double chin.
- **Wig:** fully powdered, white-grey. Raised over the forehead, with several stacked curls over the ears. Tied at the nape.
- **Costume:** polished steel cuirass with articulated arm plates. Black stock with a white lace jabot. Lace cuffs. A blue velvet mantle lined with ermine (white with black tails) falls from the shoulder.
- **Insignia:** broad red sash (Order of St Hubert) from his left shoulder to his right hip.
- **Pose and props:** half length. His right hand rests on a helmet with white ostrich plumes. Plain dark background.

### 2. Pitz, pastel, before 1795: the best colour reference
- **Face:** three-quarter view to the viewer's right, eyes on the viewer. Broad, round, fleshy face with full cheeks and a clear double chin. **Light blue eyes** with soft bags beneath. Straight, fairly long nose with a rounded tip. Small mouth with a closed, contented half-smile and pink lips. High forehead. Pale skin, rosy on the cheeks and nose.
- **Wig:** powdered white with a blue-grey cast, brushed straight back from a high hairline. **One long horizontal roll above each ear**, with looser curls below it at ear and nape. A **black ribbon** (bag or queue bow) at the back of the neck.
- **Costume:** cobalt-blue uniform coat (*Uniformrock*). **Black velvet stand collar and black velvet plastron** (lapels buttoned across the chest), both edged with thin silver piping. Dark cloth-covered buttons. White stock at the throat.
- **Insignia:** a **silver epaulette with rosettes on his right shoulder only**. A **red sash** runs from his left shoulder, and a sky-blue sash shows under its inner edge.
- **Pose:** head and shoulders, plain brown background.

### 3. Pitz type, oil, Speyer (oval)
- The same face and coat as no. 2. The museum describes it as a "white wig with side curl rolls on the broad head", with **blond own hair** showing at the ears.
- **Coat:** blue with black collar and black chest panel (*Brustlatz*). Silver edging and a row of dark buttons.
- **Insignia:** fringed silver epaulette (bullion fringe) on the right shoulder. Two sashes run **from the left shoulder to the right hip**: the red St Hubert sash with dark green edge stripes, and inside it the sky-blue St George sash with a silver-white edge. The museum names these orders: St George and St Hubert.
- The pale flecks on the collar are paint losses, not lace.

### 4. Zimmermann engraving, after 1780 (three-quarter length)
- **Face and wig:** the Pitz face, engraved: round with a double chin and a slight smile. Side rolls over the ears and a black ribbon bow at the nape.
- **Costume:** the same uniform as the Pitz type. Stand collar, dark plastron buttoned across the chest, thin piping along the edges. A **fringed epaulette with rosettes on the right shoulder**. Lace cuffs, and a sword belt with buckle.
- **Insignia:** a broad sash from the left shoulder to the right hip. **At the right hip hangs the St Hubert cross**: a Maltese cross with balls on its points and rays between the arms. Part of a breast star shows on the left chest beside the sash.
- **Pose and props:** standing, turned slightly to the viewer's left. Both hands rest on a long staff (baton or sheathed sword) over an **ermine-lined mantle**. A **crown on a tasselled cushion** stands on a pedestal behind him, in front of a draped curtain.
- The Latin caption calls him Grand Prior of the Order of St George and Grand Master of the Order of St Michael. This dates the print to after April 1780. It also says the engraving was made *juxta originale Bipontini*, after an original in Zweibrücken.

### 5. Copy after "Bauer", c. 1780, Bayerisches Nationalmuseum (black-and-white photo)
- Uniform coat with epaulettes. An **eight-pointed breast star** on the left chest. Same wig type.
- The face looks heavier and older than in Pitz. It is a provincial copy, so use it for costume only.

### 6. Mannlich, 1790: court dress (painting lost; two reproductions)
- **Face:** front view, turned slightly left. **Very corpulent.** Heavy jowls and a pronounced double chin. Heavy upper lids over large pale eyes. Straight nose with a broad, rounded tip. Small full mouth. A more sober, less smiling expression than in Pitz.
- **Wig:** powdered. Brushed back, with **two stacked rolls above each ear**.
- **Costume:** a dark **velvet court coat** (*habit à la française*) with a standing collar, large self-covered buttons and soft folds. A **lace jabot** at the throat.
- **Insignia:** a large **eight-pointed silver breast star** on the left chest. Its round centre is inscribed in two lines, probably the St Hubert motto *In Traw Vast* (not verified). Below the star hangs a white **Maltese cross with points and rays**, probably the St Hubert badge. A **small round jewel or cross on a chain** hangs from the right lapel buttonhole; it is not identified. No sash is visible.
- **Pose:** half length, in an oval. Background plain.

### 7. Pitz, 1783, full length (link only, Residenz München)
- Shown as "Generalissimus" welcoming back his Régiment Royal-Deux-Ponts from the American War of Independence.
- **Costume:** steel cuirass over **buff leather sleeves**. Buff breeches. **Black jackboots with spurs**. The red and blue order sashes. Order cross at the neck. An **ermine-lined crimson mantle**. A white waist sash with gold fringe.
- **Props:** baton, black tricorne with white trim, crown on a cushion, and a military camp behind.
- **The painting includes a Black servant in a turban.** This is a colonial-era convention of court display. A 1778 file of the Zweibrücken legation in Paris records the procurement of a Black boy for the duke (Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv, Gesandtschaft Paris 189, <https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/PCYEFA7GBZHBHB2GMSEAXSA7JYBL6NFE>). The story session should treat this with care. Do not reproduce it as decoration.

### Written descriptions (Mannlich's memoirs, ed. Stollreither 1910)
- As a youth: *"groß, von schöner Gestalt und für sein Alter sehr entwickelt"* (tall, well built, mature for his age), with unusually sharp perception and the gift of pleasing when he wished (p. 41).
- Later: *"Im allgemeinen war Karl August sehr mäßiger Natur, obgleich er im Rufe eines starken Essers stand"*. He suffered frequent indigestion and preferred his armchair to exercise (p. 394).
- The portraits show him growing visibly corpulent between c. 1775 and 1790.

## Build summary

1. **Face (constant across portraits):** broad, round head with full cheeks and a double chin. Light blue eyes (#7FA7C9). Straight nose with a rounded tip. Small closed mouth with a slight half-smile. Pale skin (#E8C0A8) with rosy cheeks (#D98C7A). Own hair blond.
2. **Body:** tall and heavy. Fairly stout around 1778, visibly corpulent by 1790. Do not make him slim.
3. **Wig:** powdered white-grey (#E9E6DF), brushed back from a high hairline. One or two horizontal side rolls above each ear. Black bag or queue ribbon (#111111) at the nape.
4. **Default outfit (Pitz uniform type, 1780s):** blue uniform coat (#2F64A0). Black velvet stand collar and plastron (#141414) with thin silver piping. Dark buttons. White stock (#F1EEE6).
5. **Insignia on the default outfit:** silver epaulette (#C9CCD1) on the right shoulder only. Two sashes from left shoulder to right hip: red St Hubert (#C8352B, green edges #2E6B3A) and sky-blue St George (#8EC3E6, white edge).
6. **Lower body (from the 1783 full-length; the bust portraits do not show it):** buff breeches (#D8C08E), black jackboots (#1A1A1A) with spurs. Optional cuirass (#9AA3AD) for a "commander" variant.
7. **State or ceremonial variant:** ermine-lined mantle (white #F4F1EA with black tails) in crimson (#8E1B23) or blue velvet (#2A3F7A). Crown on a cushion. Baton.
8. **Court-dress variant (Mannlich 1790):** dark velvet coat, lace jabot, a large silver eight-pointed breast star on the left chest, and a white cross below it.
9. **Silhouette cues for a stylized figure:** round face with a double chin, white side rolls, black-and-blue coat, and the red sash. Those four read even at a distance.

## Facts for the codex

1. Karl II. August was born on 29 October 1746 in Düsseldorf and died on 1 April 1795 in Mannheim, of a stroke. ([Wikipedia: Karl II. August](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_II._August))
2. On 5 November 1775 he succeeded his uncle, Duke Christian IV, in Pfalz-Zweibrücken. In 1774 he had married Maria Amalia of Saxony. ([Wikipedia: Karl II. August](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_II._August))
3. Their only son, Karl August Friedrich, was born on 2 March 1776 and died on 21 August 1784. ([Wikipedia: Karl II. August](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_II._August))
4. He bought the Louisenhof on the Buchenberg near Homburg and built Schloss Karlsberg there, one of the largest palace complexes of late-18th-century Central Europe. The residence moved there in 1779. ([Wikipedia: Karl II. August](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_II._August))
5. With its collections, the Karlsberg cost about 14 million guilders, roughly fourteen times the duchy's annual revenue. ([E. Weis, "Karl II. August", NDB 11, 1977](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz57323.html)) He left debts of more than 9 million guilders. ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_II._August))
6. The Karlsberg held collections of prints, weapons and pipe heads and a library. The Carlslust park had menageries. ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_II._August)) His armoury showed weapons said to have belonged to Gustav Adolf, Charles XII, Prince Eugene and Frederick the Great. (Mannlich, *Lebenserinnerungen*, ed. Stollreither 1910, pp. 392–393, [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/eindeutschermale00mann))
7. Mannlich describes a winter dining room, the "Salon Mannlich". It held flowering trees, about twenty nightingales, monkeys, parrots and a fountain with Chinese fish. The duke's motto there: *"Das Leben ist so kurz, daß man verstehen muß, es zu genießen."* (Mannlich 1910, pp. 392–393, [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/eindeutschermale00mann))
8. He told Mannlich: *"Wisse Er, mein lieber Mannlich, daß für mich nichts zu gut ist."* Bronze statues and enamelled stoves were ordered from Paris. (Mannlich 1910, p. 341, [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/eindeutschermale00mann))
9. Mannlich's verdict: strict, very hard to handle, suspicious and distrustful, demanding even the impossible; selfish, "but good at heart". Generous to the point of extravagance, yet seemingly stingy in his private life. (Mannlich 1910, pp. 339–340, [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/eindeutschermale00mann))
10. As heir to Bavaria and the Palatinate he resisted the planned exchange of Bavaria twice, in 1778/79 and 1784/85. In 1785 he joined the Fürstenbund. ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_II._August); [NDB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz57323.html))
11. He held the Order of St Hubert from the age of three months (2 February 1747). He became Grand Master of the Order of St Michael in 1778 and Grand Prior of the Order of St George in 1780. ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_II._August))
12. He kept par force hunts with large packs of hounds, which his subjects had to feed. The nickname "Hundskarl" was popularised only in 1923, by Fritz Schunck, so it is not a contemporary name. ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_II._August))
13. In 1789 the inhabitants of Schloss Karlsberg dedicated a silver medal to his recovery from smallpox. (Münzkabinett Dresden BNB3704, [DDB](https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/GYWHWHRYQMRBR44REIBFHQDJ4CMH63SB))
14. Warned by Nicolaus Pfeiffer, he fled the Karlsberg in February 1793 before French troops. Wikipedia gives 9 February; the NDB gives 3 February (**uncertain**). French troops burned the palace on 28 July 1793. ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_II._August); [NDB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz57323.html))
15. **Numbering:** in Zweibrücken he is Karl II. August. Bavarian and Wittelsbach-family records often call him "Karl III. August", as on the Foto Marburg cards here. Both refer to the same man. ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_II._August))

## Open questions

- **Pitz dates.** The Munich pastel is dated "vor 1795" (older records: 1795). The Speyer oil is dated c. 1790 by the museum and c. 1780 on Commons. The type could come from anywhere in the 1780s.
- **"Bauer".** The archive card of the Nationalmuseum copy names a painter "Bauer". This may be a misreading of Nikolaus Lauer, who also painted the duke; not verified.
- **The 1790 breast star.** It is probably St Hubert (an inscribed centre fits *In Traw Vast*), but the book reproductions are too coarse to be sure. The lapel jewel is unidentified.
- **The 1783 full-length.** It is the richest costume source, but it is link-only. The Commons file looks like a photo of a printed reproduction with unclear rights. An official photo from the Bayerische Schlösserverwaltung would settle this.
