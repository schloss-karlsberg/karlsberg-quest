# Johann Christian von Mannlich (1741–1822)

Reference for the speaker `mannlich` (stylized 3D figure, codex). Mannlich was court painter and building director (*Baudirektor*) to Duke Karl II. August, and he ran the building of the Karlsberg. In the game period (1778–1793) he is **37 to 52 years old**.

Licence and credit for every image: [`sources.json`](sources.json). The images are not MIT-licensed. Read [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) first.

> **Gap: no portrait from his Karlsberg years has been found.** The adult likeness that survives is the Seele portrait of 1808, made when he was about 67. The only other image is a youth portrait from about 1756. NDB mentions a self-portrait with his wife from 1778, and the 1910 edition mentions a lithograph of the couple, but neither could be found online. The 1778–1793 figure therefore has to be **extrapolated**: take the bone structure from Seele and the build from the youth portrait, and use the dress conventions of the period.

## Appearance by portrait

### 1. Seele, 1808 (Neue Pinakothek): the primary face reference
- **Age:** 66–67, painted in the year he was ennobled.
- **Face:** shown in three-quarter view to the viewer's left, eyes glancing further left. A **long, narrow, lean face**.
  - High forehead with horizontal lines. Arched, rather high brows.
  - **Brown eyes**, a little hooded.
  - A **long, prominent, straight nose** with a rounded tip.
  - Thin, closed lips with a faint, alert smile. **Deep nasolabial folds**, hollow cheeks.
  - A firm, slightly pointed chin with a small jowl below.
  - Warm, slightly ruddy complexion.
  - The expression is lively, sharp and humorous.
- **Hair:** his **own hair, unpowdered, mid-brown, short and wavy**, in the Empire fashion. It is tousled over the forehead and covers the tops of his ears. There is no wig and no queue.
- **Costume:**
  - A dark navy, almost black, cloth coat with a **high turned-up collar** and broad lapels.
  - The white shirt collar stands up to his cheeks. A white neckcloth is wound high around the throat, and a **frilled shirt jabot** shows below it.
- **Insignia:** an order ribbon in the left lapel buttonhole. The ribbon is blue-green with white edges and carries a gold bar with a small white cross or star. This is probably the knight's cross of the Civil Merit Order of the Bavarian Crown, which he received in 1808. That order's ribbon is blue with white side stripes, and knights wear it in the buttonhole.
- **Pose:** head and shoulders, against a warm brown sketchy background. No props.

### 2. and 3. Seele type, Berlin family version (1910 plate; photo c. 1940)
- The **same face and costume**, but a different and larger canvas (83 × 67 cm against 55.5 × 44.2 cm). The pose is the same three-quarter view to the left. The plain light background helps with the **profile line**: long straight nose, thin lips, pointed chin, and the high collar.
- The 1910 editor calls it a Seele painting. The Foto Marburg card of about 1940 calls it a self-portrait. See the open questions.

### 4. Hoffnass engraving after Seele (after 1806)
- Black-and-white line version of the same face, slightly idealised: tousled hair, high collar, jabot, ribbon in the buttonhole.
- The inscription gives his office: *"Director aller Königl. baierischen Kunst-Samlungen"*.

### 5. Konrad Mannlich, "Drawing boy", c. 1756 (age c. 15)
- **Face:** in profile to the viewer's right. A youthful, oval face with a **straight nose**, a soft chin and a fresh complexion.
- **Hair:** **own brown hair**, unpowdered, brushed back and tied at the nape.
- **Costume:** a brown cloth coat with white shirt cuffs showing.
- **Pose and props:** seated at a table, **drawing** on a sheet with a pen or chalk holder by the light of an oil lamp. Books and drawing tools lie beside him.
- The museum's own title is neutral. The identification with the young Mannlich comes from the gallery display.

### Written description
- In his memoirs Mannlich recalls receiving a coat in 1766 and remarks that two young men of his build could have fitted into it comfortably. He was **very slim** as a young man (Mannlich, *Lebenserinnerungen*, ed. Stollreither 1910, p. 77, [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/eindeutschermale00mann)).
- Seele still shows a lean man at 67. Keep him **lean throughout**.

## Build summary

1. **Face (from Seele 1808, made younger):** long, narrow, lean face. Long, prominent, straight nose with a rounded tip. Arched brows, **brown eyes** (#5A3A22). Thin lips with a faint wry smile, a firm pointed chin. For age 37–52, soften the folds and keep the hollow cheeks.
2. **Build:** slim and upright, the opposite of the round duke. Be careful: the memoir and Seele give "lean", but his height is not recorded.
3. **Hair, 1778–1793 (extrapolated, no portrait):** a court official of the 1780s wears his hair powdered (#E3DFD6), with side rolls and a black queue ribbon or bag (#111111), usually his own hair rather than a full wig. His natural hair is mid-brown (#6B5340), so let it show at the temples or nape. For a "today" scene or a late epilogue, use Seele's unpowdered short hair.
4. **Outfit, 1778–1793 (extrapolated):** a civilian frock coat (*habit*), knee-length with a stand collar. Plain, dark, sober cloth: deep brown (#5A3B26) or dark blue-black (#1E2433, the colour of the 1808 coat). Under it a lighter waistcoat (buff #CDB991 or dove grey #A9A79F), a white jabot and cuffs (#F1EEE6), knee breeches matching the coat, white stockings, black buckle shoes. A small sword is optional.
5. **No orders before 1808.** He received the Bavarian Crown order only in 1808, so his Karlsberg figure wears **no insignia**. This marks him as a commoner artist-official next to the duke.
6. **Props:** porte-crayon (chalk holder) or brush, a portfolio, and rolled architectural plans of the Karlsberg. These fit both his roles: painter, and director of building works and of the drawing school.
7. **Silhouette cues:** narrow and upright, dark coat, white jabot, long nose in profile, portfolio under the arm.

## Facts for the codex

1. Mannlich was born in Strasbourg on 2 October 1741 and died in Munich on 3 January 1822. **The older ADB gives 4 October 1740.** ([F. Roland, "Mannlich, Johann Christian von", NDB 16, 1990](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz57830.html); [H. Holland, ADB 20, 1884](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Mannlich,_Johann_Christian_von))
2. His father, Konrad Mannlich, was court painter at Zweibrücken and gave him his first lessons. Duke Christian IV then sent him to train in Mannheim. ([ADB](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Mannlich,_Johann_Christian_von))
3. In 1763 he went to Paris with Duke Christian IV and became a pupil of François Boucher. From 1767 to 1771 he studied in Rome as a pensioner of the French Academy, copying Raphael against Boucher's advice. ([ADB](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Mannlich,_Johann_Christian_von))
4. On 27 June 1771 the Academy of Fine Arts in Parma made him a member. ([ADB](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Mannlich,_Johann_Christian_von))
5. In 1772, after his father's death, he became first court painter, gallery inspector and director of the new drawing school in Zweibrücken. ([NDB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz57830.html); [ADB](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Mannlich,_Johann_Christian_von))
6. Under Karl II. August he directed the duke's building works, including the Karlsberg. In 1788 he was given the direction of all the fine arts in the duchy. ([NDB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz57830.html))
7. He married in 1777. (Mannlich 1910, table of contents, p. 345, [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/eindeutschermale00mann))
8. In 1793 he saved the art. Wikipedia: before French revolutionary troops destroyed the Karlsberg in July 1793, Mannlich and his pupil Peter Ferdinand Deurer rescued its painting collection, library, arms collection, furniture and panelling. ADB: he took the Zweibrücken gallery to safety in Mannheim. The paintings later formed part of the basis of the Alte Pinakothek. ([Wikipedia: Johann Christian von Mannlich](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Christian_von_Mannlich); [ADB](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Mannlich,_Johann_Christian_von))
9. In Munich, from 1799, he was central director of the Bavarian painting galleries. ([NDB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz57830.html))
10. In 1808 he received the Civil Merit Order of the Bavarian Crown. For Bavarian subjects the order carried personal nobility, so from 1808 he was "von Mannlich". ([ADB](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Mannlich,_Johann_Christian_von); [Wikipedia: Verdienstorden der Bayerischen Krone](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verdienstorden_der_Bayerischen_Krone); [Wikipedia: Mannlich](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Christian_von_Mannlich))
11. He was among the first to use lithography to reproduce art: the prints of the royal drawings collection from 1810, and the paintings of the Munich and Schleissheim galleries from about 1816/17. **The NDB and ADB dates differ slightly.** ([ADB](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Mannlich,_Johann_Christian_von); [NDB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz57830.html))
12. In 1802 he published a study of the customs, dress and weapons of the ancient peoples. The ADB calls him a forerunner of costume historians. ([ADB](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Mannlich,_Johann_Christian_von))
13. His memoirs, written in French, are among the most important sources on the Karlsberg. A German edition by Eugen Stollreither appeared in 1910. ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Christian_von_Mannlich); [1910 edition, archive.org](https://archive.org/details/eindeutschermale00mann))
14. His grave in Munich's Alter Südfriedhof was destroyed in the Second World War; a memorial plate was set up in 1972. A grammar school in Homburg bears his name and has a statue of him. ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Christian_von_Mannlich))

## Open questions

- **Seele or self-portrait?** The 1910 edition credits Seele with the Berlin family painting. The Foto Marburg card of about 1940 calls the same picture (83 × 67 cm, owned by v. Mannlich-Lehmann, Berlin) a self-portrait. It is a second, larger version of the Munich Seele of 1808. Which hand painted it is open.
- **Which Hoffnass?** The Rijksmuseum names Johann Wilhelm Hoffnass (1727–1795). That cannot be right, because the inscription uses the royal Bavarian title, which only existed from 1806. The Deutsches Museum names Lorenz Hoffnass, which fits better.
- **The youth portrait.** The identification of the "drawing boy" as the young J. C. Mannlich rests on the Schwarzenacker display, not on the Bavarian State Painting Collections' title.
- **Missing 1778 portrait.** NDB lists a self-portrait with his wife dated 1778. If the Münchner Stadtmuseum (Maillinger collection) or a private owner can supply it, it should replace the extrapolated look in Build summary points 3 and 4.
